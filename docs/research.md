## 1. The real timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Autumn 1844 | Emerson buys a tract on Walden Pond (to protect the woods). Thoreau builds there with his permission. |
| Late Mar 1845 ✔ | Borrows an axe, starts cutting white pines. |
| Mid-Apr ✔ | Buys James Collins's shanty for $4.25, takes it apart for boards. Digs cellar (6×7 ft, "two hours' work"). |
| Early May ✔ | Frame raised with help from neighbours. |
| **4 July 1845** ✔ | Moves in — boarded and roofed, not finished. |
| Summer 1845 | Bean-field (2.5 acres), day labour, surveying, writing. |
| Oct–Nov 1845 ✔ | Gathers nuts, builds the chimney, plastering ("It was now November"). Pond freezes 22 Dec. |
| Winter 1845–46 | Firewood, ice, visitors, winter animals. |
| **23–24 July 1846** | Arrested by constable Sam Staples for unpaid poll tax; one night in jail; someone (probably Aunt Maria) pays; he's released next day. Not in *Walden* itself — it became "Resistance to Civil Government". |
| Second year | Smaller farm (⅓ acre spaded) — he'd learned he needed less. |
| **6 Sept 1847** | Leaves. Total stay: **2 years, 2 months, 2 days** (not 2.5 — worth deciding if we round up). |

*Walden* compresses two years into one cycle of seasons. We don't have to.

**Eight-month household ledger (4 July → 1 March)** ✔

- Food **$8.74** — rice 1.73½, molasses 1.73, rye meal 1.04¾, Indian meal 0.99¾, flour 0.88, sugar 0.80, lard 0.65, apples 0.25, dried apple 0.22, pork 0.22, sweet potatoes 0.10, pumpkin 0.06, salt 0.03, watermelon 0.02 ("Yes, I did eat $8.74, all told" ≈ 27¢/week)
- Clothing $8.40¾ · Oil + utensils $2.00 · House $28.12½ · Farm $14.72½
- **All outgoes: $61.99¾**
- Income: farm produce $23.44 + day-labour $13.34 = **$36.78** → balance $25.21¾
- Bean-field: 12 bu beans, 18 bu potatoes; spent $14.72½, sold $23.44, **net $8.71½** (+$4.50 of produce on hand). Yellow corn and turnips failed.
- Claim: ~**6 weeks of labour a year** pays for everything; a day-labourer needs 30–40 days.

**The philosophy that should drive the mechanics** ✔
- *"The cost of a thing is the amount of what I will call life which is required to be exchanged for it."* → every purchase has a **time cost**.
- The four necessaries: **Food, Shelter, Clothing, Fuel.**
- A typical Concord house ≈ 10–15 years of a labourer's wages. Farmers are "serfs of the soil."
- "The mass of men lead lives of quiet desperation." / "Improved means to an unimproved end" (railroad, telegraph).
- Furniture: a bed, table, desk, **three chairs**, a 3-inch mirror. *"None is so poor that he need sit on a pumpkin."*

## 4. Myth vs. reality — fertile ground for the "second voice"

The inconsistencies between Thoreau's *ideal* and Thoreau's *actual life* are the best material for the inner-voice mechanic. He can be a bit defensive about them.

- Cabin was **< ½ mile** from the railroad and the Concord road; the nearest neighbour ~1 mile. He went to town "every day or two."
- His **mother did his laundry**; he sometimes ate at home and at Emerson's (Sunday dinners). He hosted an annual melon party.
- He lived on **Emerson's land**, did odd jobs/carpentry in return. Called himself a "squatter."
- Income came from **surveying, carpentry, day-labour, lecturing, and the family pencil business** (he improved the graphite process). Not a freeloader.
- Not anti-technology: used trains to reach Boston libraries, owned a telescope and microscope. Objected to technology *governing* life (farmers set clocks by train stops).
- Went there to **write** (A Week on the Concord and Merrimack Rivers was drafted there, and Walden begun). Channing called the cabin "a wooden inkstand."
- Friends: Emerson, Alcott (Sunday evenings), Ellery Channing, Hawthorne.

## 5. Prior art

- **Walden, a game** (USC Game Innovation Lab / Tracy Fullerton, 2017; Games for Change Game of the Year). First-person, you can't die (you faint). Two axes: **survival** (the four necessaries) and **inspiration** (sounds, solitude, reading, visitors). Mixed reception — often called slow. *Lesson: the survival-vs-meaning tension is the right core; our twist is the dialogue-driven, LLM-powered voice, which they didn't have.*
- **Disco Elysium**: skills as inner *voices* that interject, argue with each other, and gate dialogue options (passive and active checks). *Lesson: the second-voice idea has strong precedent; it works because voices have distinct personality and are sometimes wrong.*
- **Reference project (Lecture 18)**: LLM-as-Dungeon-Master over a server-owned world.
